Findings — ordered by how much they hurt on a phone

01

The hero carousel is doing four jobs and losing all four

In Hero.tsx a single 4:3 frame at max-h-68vh carries a rotating still, a YouTube iframe that dissolves in at 2.2s, three stacked scrims, wordmark art, a metadata line, two pills, and five dots — all inside AnimatePresence on an 8-second timer. On a 390pt screen that means the title changes under your thumb while you're reading it, the dots are hidden below sm so rotation has no visible control, and the trailer's 135% overscan crops the logo art it just faded up.

Fix — stop auto-rotating on mobile. One featured title, manual paging, trailer only after a deliberate tap. Continue Watching takes the top of the page instead, which is what a returning user came for.

02

Player fullscreen has three fallback paths and no single source of truth

ReliablePlayer.tsx tries webkitEnterFullscreen on the video, then container requestFullscreen, then a CSS .player-cinema-mode. Each path produces a different chrome, and the fullscreenchange sync clears cinema mode whenever the real fullscreen element isn't the shell — so the third path silently cancels itself. Meanwhile the player card is h-svh inside a scrollable page, so on iOS the address bar collapse resizes the video mid-playback.

Fix — the player is a modal route that owns the viewport at 100dvh from the moment it opens. No fullscreen button at all: portrait is the compact player, rotating to landscape *is* fullscreen. One state, no fallbacks.

03

Player controls collide because four systems place things absolutely

The exhausted-servers banner, the no-captions prompt, StuckStreamToast and the header controls each pin themselves with their own absolute + z-50 and their own safe-area class. Two of them share .player-safe-bottom and stack on top of each other. globals.css then patches the symptom by display:none-ing auxiliary controls in landscape and fullscreen — which hides the server switcher exactly when a stream dies.

Fix — one control layer, a three-row grid (top bar / centre transport / bottom bar) with a dedicated notification slot above the scrubber. Nothing is ever hidden by media query; alerts queue in one slot instead of overlapping.

04

The wordmark is hidden in a rail that has to be hovered

ImmersiveAppShell.tsx renders BrandLogo at w-[190px] inside a rail clipped to w-20 with overflow-hidden, so at rest you see the first ~48px of the letterforms. On mobile the dock drops the brand entirely. The product has no persistent identity anywhere.

Fix — a monogram lockup that is legible at 20px collapsed and expands to the full wordmark, plus the wordmark in the mobile home header where a returning user actually looks.

05

Seven dock items, two of which go nowhere

site.tsx lists nine nav items; the mobile dock shows the seven that aren't desktopOnly. At 36–60px each inside a max-w-[calc(100vw-1rem)] pill, the magnifying hover effect is fighting for space it doesn't have — and macOS-dock magnification has no meaning on a touchscreen where there is no cursor to be near.

Fix — five tabs: Home · Search · Browse · Anime · My Space. Movies, TV and Categories become segments inside Browse. No magnification; a sliding indicator instead.

06

Every poster wears the same permanent scrim

PosterCard.tsx paints a 60%-tall gradient at 85% resting opacity over the bottom of every card. It solves legibility, but a rail of twelve cards becomes twelve identical dark-bottomed rectangles — the art can't carry the page when 60% of it is veiled. Hover reveal is also dead weight on touch.

Fix — clean art in rails, title and metadata below the card. Keep the scrim only where text must sit on the art (billboard, continue-watching, TV rows).

07

Long-press for a hover preview is an invisible affordance

On mobile, PosterCard opens a Vaul drawer after a 300ms press with a 12ms haptic — a good interaction that nothing on screen advertises. Desktop gets the same content in a HeroUI Tooltip, which still can't reposition away from the viewport edge.

Fix — keep long-press, but surface the same actions in an explicit overflow affordance on the card, and let the quick-look sheet be the place watchlist / watch-party / episode actions live.

08

Written, wired to nothing

useExtractColors.ts is fully implemented and imported nowhere. WatchProviders.tsx exists and the data is fetched. Sports and Sparks render "Soon" chips in the rail. That's three finished capabilities the UI never shows.

Fix — ambient poster-colour theming on every detail page and player (that's the signature you picked), providers surfaced under the play row, and Soon states designed rather than chipped.
